# How NeyborHuud Talks

The rule for every word in the app: **write the way a friendly Lagos neighbour talks, not the way a bank letter reads.**

Nigerian everyday English, simple and warm, with the local expressions almost every Nigerian uses. Not textbook English, not heavy slang.

---

## What the research says

- **Clear and honest beats clever.** Kuda's writers aim to "write the way we would talk to a friend: honest, respectful, and human", and say "clarity beats cleverness every time". Nigeria is a low-trust market where money is involved, so people want plain, honest words, not wordplay. ([Kuda case study](https://contra.com/p/3oULo636-kuda-bank-app))
- **Say what will actually happen.** "Transfer pending" scares people. "Your transfer is being processed and should reflect within 10 minutes" calms them. ([Vanguard, UX writing in Nigerian fintech](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2025/09/ux-writing-as-infrastructure-for-transparent-algorithms-in-financial-services/amp/))
- **Local phrasing builds connection.** App-localisation research for Nigeria repeatedly finds that Nigerian English expressions make users feel the product is theirs, and that direct translations miss the words people actually use. ([AppDrift Nigeria](https://appdrift.co/app-store-optimization/nigeria/finance))
- **Pidgin reaches almost everyone.** Around 75 million Nigerians speak Pidgin as a second language and 3–5 million as a first language. Almost every Nigerian understands it, and Pidgin adverts are widely understood across education levels. ([RWS](https://www.rws.com/blog/can-pidgin-languages-be-used-for-marketing-and-advertising), [UNIBEN](https://repository.uniben.edu/taxonomy/term/13694), [Translated](https://imminent.translated.com/data-index/nigeria))
- **But English is the shared base.** Nigeria ranks 3rd in Africa for English proficiency, and adult literacy is about 70%. So the base is simple English, flavoured with expressions everyone knows. ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1268372/literacy-rate-in-nigeria), [Translated](https://imminent.translated.com/data-index/nigeria))
- **Nigerian English has its own greetings.** "Well done" greets someone at work, "Sorry" shows sympathy when something bad happens even if it wasn't your fault, and "Happy birthday in arrears" means belated. These map to Hausa *sannu*, Yoruba *pele* / *eku ise* and Igbo *ndo*. ([Farooq Kperogi](https://www.farooqkperogi.com/2012/11/top-10-peculiar-salutations-in-nigerian.html), [Kperogi](https://www.farooqkperogi.com/2018/06/nigerian-words-and-expressions-that-are.html))
- **These words are real English now.** The Oxford English Dictionary added 29 Nigerian English words in January 2020, including *next tomorrow*, *danfo*, *okada*, *tokunbo*, *buka*, *mama put*, *send-forth* and *barbing salon*. ([NativeMag](https://thenativemag.com/oxford-english-dictionary-nigerian-english/), [Cherwell](https://cherwell.org/2020/02/01/nigerian-english-words-added-to-oxford-english-dictionary/))
- **Everyday words differ from British or American English.** *Light* means electricity, *go-slow* means traffic jam, *flash* means a deliberate missed call, *chop* means eat, *dash* means give. ([Kperogi](https://www.farooqkperogi.com/2012/01/nigerias-unique-telephonic-vocabulary.html))

## The three levels

| Level | Where | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Plain and calm** | SOS, kidnapping, emergencies, payments, escrow, ID checks, legal text | "SOS sent. Your guardians have your location." Short, clear, no slang. |
| **2. Everyday Nigerian (the default)** | Greetings, feed, posts, chat, marketplace, empty screens, tips, small errors | "Good morning, Tunde." "Wetin dey happen around you today?" "Price am." |
| **3. Full Pidgin** | Only when the user picks Pidgin in Settings | "How body? Wetin dey sup for your area?" |

When in doubt, go one level calmer.

---

## Rules

1. **Short sentences.** One idea each. Most people read on small phones with costly data.
2. **Talk to one person:** "you", "your street", "your people".
3. **Light seasoning, not soup.** At most one local expression per message. "Rain don start for Lekki o. Stay dry." is good; three slang words in one line is too much.
4. **Use the expressions everyone knows**, from Lagos to Kano to Port Harcourt (word bank below). Avoid deep street slang that changes every year.
5. **Never joke about danger, money or loss.**
6. **Say what will happen next and when**, especially for money: "Your money is held safely until you confirm you've received the item."
7. **No blame in errors.** "Network don do us strong thing. Try again." not "Invalid request."
8. **Use Nigerian reality:** naira (₦), estates, gate man, landlord, light, generator, go-slow, danfo, okada, keke, Lagos, Abuja and PH place names.
9. **Never use Americanisms:** apartment → flat or house; zip code; cell phone → phone; vacation → holiday; gas (fuel) → fuel or petrol; neighbor → neighbour.
10. **Sentence case for buttons and titles:** "Make a post", not "Make A Post".

## Word bank

**Use freely (understood everywhere):**

| Expression | Meaning | Example in the app |
|---|---|---|
| o | soft emphasis | "Stay safe o." |
| abeg | please (soft request) | "Abeg, add a photo so buyers can see it." |
| wetin | what | "Wetin dey happen around you?" |
| no wahala | no problem | "No wahala, you can change it later." |
| sharp sharp | quickly | "Reply sharp sharp to win the deal." |
| well done | greeting for someone working or doing good | "Well done! Your report helped 12 neighbours." |
| sorry | sympathy | "Sorry about your loss. Your neighbours have been told." |
| you don reach? | did you arrive safely? | Safe-trip check-in |
| price am | make an offer, bargain | Marketplace offer button |
| buy am | buy it | Marketplace buy button |
| light | electricity | "Light don come back for Yaba?" |
| go-slow | traffic jam | "Go-slow on Third Mainland Bridge" |
| gist | news, chat | "Share the gist with your neighbours" |
| area, street, estate | neighbourhood | "Your area" |
| oga, madam | respectful address | Only in examples, never for the user themselves |
| my people | family, friends | "Tell your people you've arrived." |

**Use with care, casual moments only:** omo (surprise), e choke (impressive), no shaking (no worries), japa (relocate), wahala (trouble).

**Never use in the app:** insults, ethnic jokes, religious assumptions, scam slang (yahoo, maga, "format"), anything mocking poverty.

## By moment

### Greetings
| Time | Say |
|---|---|
| Morning (until 12) | Good morning, Tunde |
| Afternoon (12–4) | Good afternoon, Tunde |
| Evening and night | Good evening, Tunde |

Never "Good night" as a greeting: in Nigeria it means goodbye, said when someone leaves or goes to bed.

### Making a post
- Day: "Wetin dey happen around you today?"
- Night: "Anything happening around you tonight?"
- Empty feed: "Nobody don post for your area yet. Start the gist!"
- Posted: "Your post is live. Your neighbours can see it now."

### Weather (Nigerian weather, not snow)
- Rain: "Rain don start for Lekki. Stay dry o."
- Storm: "Heavy rain for Lekki. Stay inside if you can."
- Hot: "Sun dey hot for Lekki today. Drink water."
- Harmattan or haze: "Harmattan haze for Lekki. Drive carefully."
- Clear: "Fine weather for Lekki today."

### Buying and selling
- Buy: "Buy am"
- Offer: "Price am"
- Chat seller: "Chat the seller"
- Escrow explained: "Your money stays safe with NeyborHuud until you confirm you got the item."
- Sold: "E don sell! Well done."

### Chat
- Empty inbox: "No chats yet. Say hello to a neighbour."
- Arrived safely: "You don reach? Tell your people you're home."

### Errors
- Network: "Network don do us strong thing. Check your data and try again."
- Server: "Something went wrong on our side. Please try again."
- Not allowed: "You can't do that here. Ask the group admin."

### Safety (level 1, always plain)
- SOS: "SOS sent. Your guardians and nearby responders have your location."
- Report: "Report received. We'll let you know when neighbours confirm it."
- Kidnapping alert: plain English only; no expressions at all.

## Process

1. Rewrite English text screen by screen using this guide.
2. Move it into `pwa/src/lib/i18n.tsx` so every language can be switched.
3. Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo and Hausa: draft, then **native-speaker review** before release. Safety text is never machine-translated alone.
4. Test new wording with real residents during the pilot; keep what people understand and act on.
